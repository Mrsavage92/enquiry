# Enquiry: pre-launch decision brief

Date: 7 September 2026 (Australia/Brisbane)
Status: RECOMMENDATION AND DRAFT COPY. Not an activated offer, public announcement, engineering authorisation or release sign-off.
Repository reference checked: `Mrsavage92/enquiry`, main at `41d9d3371a5fa9a2a29552faea2506f65080dd4d`.

## Decision

Launch the early-access waitlist as soon as a small, safe, truthful public signup experience is verified. Build the audience while the product develops. Start selected businesses on free private beta only when the relevant product safety gate passes. Do not postpone useful beta or paid access to reach an arbitrary list size.

Keep 10,000 genuinely interested, verified subscribers as the growth ambition. Treat it as a goal, not a forecast, a prerequisite for launch or a claim about existing demand. Measure business fit and continued interest separately.

The first announcement should make the buyer recognise the problem and know where to join. It should not explain the whole product, discuss the founder's previous launches, publish the entire business model or recount uncorrected product defects.

## Corrections to the preceding advice

1. Do not require the entire visual redesign or all ten product reviews before collecting waitlist interest. Verify the isolated public surface and preserve the separate beta gate.
2. Do not lead the first announcement with AuditHQ or a story about building in silence. Use that lesson internally; lead with the buyer's problem.
3. Do not introduce conflicting benefits such as 20% for a year, 30% indefinitely, vague price protection and referral multipliers. Use one public offer with explicit scope.
4. Do not hide an approved incentive for an arbitrary number of posts. Keep the first post short, but put the offer and terms on the signup page from the beginning.
5. Do not assume Product Hunt is the main route to prospective service-business customers. Use founder distribution plus specific buyer communities and partners; treat launch directories as supplementary.
6. Do not turn Adam's personal profile into an Enquiry-only feed. Use two Enquiry posts within a suggested three-post total weekly cadence.
7. Do not equate a large mailing list with active customers, verified product accuracy or commercial demand.

## Release structure

| Stage | Public meaning | Access |
|---|---|---|
| Early-access list | Follow the build, reserve the approved early-supporter benefit, express testing interest | Email-first signup, no product account or payment |
| Private beta | Invited businesses test the supported workflow free and give honest feedback | Small, selected cohorts; invite terms explain scope and usage limits |
| Paid early access | Supported product with an explicit price and limitations | Voluntary paid subscription, never automatic conversion from free beta |
| Wider launch | Market to a wider audience with demonstrable product value | Availability matches advertised capability and commercial terms |

The list can keep growing while beta starts with five suitable businesses. Expand to around 25 only when repeated use, support load and correctness justify it. These are operating steps, not guaranteed capacity or calendar commitments. Preserve asynchronous onboarding and feedback; no required interviews, discovery calls or weekly beta calls.

## Proposed early-supporter offer

Recommendation: everyone who verifies their waitlist email before the publicly announced close of pre-launch enrolment reserves 20% off the standard base subscription price of an eligible self-serve plan, while that subscription stays active. Invited testers use the product free during private beta. A waitlist place does not guarantee a beta invitation.

The percentage is a strategic proposal, not a validated economic optimum. Before publishing, approve the offer after modelling model/API, email, infrastructure and support costs at the discounted price. No sale price or alleged dollar saving should be invented before a genuine standard price exists.

Suggested precise terms to approve before activation:

- One early-supporter entitlement per verified email/business workspace, non-transferable and not multiplied by duplicate signups.
- A 60-day claim window starts only when that subscriber receives an actual paid-access invitation for a supported plan/market, with price and claim deadline shown. Never start the clock merely because a public launch happened while the subscriber cannot access the product.
- Discount applies to the eligible plan's then-current standard base subscription price and its included usage. Additional usage, separately priced add-ons, bespoke services and enterprise contracts are excluded clearly beside the offer, not hidden behind a contradictory headline.
- The same percentage continues across eligible self-serve plan changes. It is not a fixed monetary price, an unlimited-usage promise or a promise about every future product.
- Publish what cancellation and billing failure mean. A suggested billing grace period is 14 days; no silent loss of the benefit while a recoverable payment issue is being resolved. Do not retrospectively shrink an earned benefit by relabelling a replacement equivalent plan.
- Do not stack other promotional coupons unless expressly stated. Explain the treatment of annual billing before selling.
- Marketing unsubscribe does not erase an earned entitlement or re-enable marketing. Users can later claim through a supported verification flow without having to re-consent to promotional email.
- Free beta requires no card and never auto-converts to paid. Before any transition, disclose the price and give at least 30 days' notice, then seek explicit purchase consent.
- Beta can have disclosed usage limits and can end. Do not advertise free unlimited access or an indefinite funded service.

Do not publish this offer until its actual terms and recording mechanism are ready. Until then, a no-discount teaser can invite people to follow the build without implying an earned discount.

## Audience and message

Horizontal product; focused initial distribution.

Start with two acquisition groups:

1. Wedding/event and creative suppliers: photographers, bridal makeup businesses and similar operators whose enquiries require scope, timing or service decisions. Adam has relevant first-hand context through Louise, but this is not permission to use her client data, mailing list or endorsement.
2. Small creative/professional service businesses: studios, consultants and agencies with variable-scope work, especially where founder LinkedIn distribution can reach owners.

Treat this as a testable acquisition choice, not a final exclusion of trades. Keep suitable enquiries from planned trades and other service businesses on the list; widen targeted distribution when evidence and supported workflows justify it. Do not lead with buyers seeking emergency dispatch, sensitive regulated workflows or routine fixed-slot booking only.

Start beta with the geography and currency the product actually supports. Collect optional country after signup; do not promise international paid availability on a date that has not been committed.

Positioning for the first campaign:

> Enquiry is being built to reduce the work between a customer asking and a booking being agreed, using how that particular service business operates.

The attraction is less administration and a useful next step. The differentiator is business-specific judgement, not simply producing a faster generic reply. Describe ambition as ambition; do not imply connected inboxes, automatic follow-up or reliable booking decisions are live before they are verified.

Keep the existing email-first flow. Do not build a new platform, onboarding wizard or reward system just to collect interest.

Suggested copy structure, with offer text withheld until approved:

Do not publicly promise that the reader will get a paid product on a fixed date. Do not show fabricated countdowns, queue positions, customer logos, usage metrics or social proof. Separate "sign in" for existing users from the prospect signup route.

## Signup and email lifecycle

The inspected `joinWaitlist` handler stores the email, attribution and an event. That is useful capture infrastructure, not proof of the full confirmation, benefit-reservation or mailing process.

Desired sequence:

1. Submit email with transparent consent wording. No product login, card or required questionnaire.
2. Show "Check your email to confirm your place" and send a non-promotional verification message with an expiring, rate-limited token. Permit resend with abuse controls.
3. On verification, record confirmation, original entitlement/terms version and immutable first-touch source. Never let a duplicate attempt overwrite an existing benefit or first-touch evidence.
4. Send a concise welcome: what the list means, current stage, any approved reserved benefit and update cadence. Never label this a beta-access grant.
5. Offer optional business type, country, enquiry volume, pain and beta-interest fields. Skipping does not forfeit the offer.
6. Provide a straightforward unsubscribe and suppress future marketing. Preserve benefit entitlement separately.
7. Send useful build notes approximately fortnightly, only when there is meaningful content.

Record collection and consent version, confirmation, unsubscribe, terms/offer entitlement, first/latest attribution and actual invitation state separately. Avoid a public subscriber-list API. Keep all real subscriber data and secrets out of the public repo.

The current form says "We'll only email about Enquiry access". Do not assume that consent automatically covers a new promotional build newsletter. Align new-signup wording with the intended communication, and review existing consent before expanding what existing subscribers receive. Do not import AuditHQ subscribers into Enquiry without an appropriate permission basis.

Before growth, test branded sending, authentication, real inbox receipt, verification, duplicate submission, a failed-mail retry, optional qualification, unsubscribe and the original source/offer surviving retries. A successful database insert alone is not a launch-ready funnel.

## Content cadence

Suggested total: three personal LinkedIn posts weekly, of which two can feature Enquiry. This replaces the earlier suggestion that Enquiry might take over all three.

- One problem/decision post: show a recognisable service-business situation and what matters.
- One short progress/proof post: show one supported behaviour and its practical effect. Label prototypes and examples.
- Remaining personal post: Adam's wider operational/AI experience or another relevant topic.

Vary the entry point: customer changes scope; owner needs one clarification; same words mean different things for different businesses; what the owner still controls. Do not publish eight variations of "I'm building something".

A fixed bug can become a useful engineering lesson when appropriate. Do not lead the buyer campaign with unresolved defects, blame a model for deterministic application code, or claim that a review finding is already fixed.

Fortnightly email: one meaningful change, one image or brief example, one optional question. No filler progress or invented success metrics. Do not send a long changelog. Suitable question: "Which enquiry do you find yourself rewriting most often?" Do not solicit identifiable customer content by default.

## The route to 10,000

Count verified, unique, subscribed people; separately report service-business fit, business/workspace duplicates, geography, beta interest and ongoing engagement. An unqualified subscriber is "unknown fit", not automatically poor fit. Remove bots and duplicated addresses from headline figures. Do not require a company domain to qualify a genuine sole trader.

Illustration, not a forecast or benchmark:

- 62,500 relevant landing-page visitors.
- 20% submit an email: 12,500 submissions.
- 80% confirm: 10,000 verified subscribers.
- If only 60% of those demonstrate target-business fit, there are 6,000 known-qualified subscribers, not 10,000 qualified businesses.

A 10,000-person list requires distribution at a different scale from one founder announcement. Seek repeatable sources of relevant visits and partner introductions before adding complicated referral rewards or buying traffic.

Progression:

- First 50 qualified subscribers: establish a repeatable problem/message, recruit suitable pilot candidates.
- 250: identify repeatable acquisition sources and send useful ongoing updates.
- 1,000: test a simple optional share link and a few measurable collaborations. Referrals must not supersede beta suitability or earn indefinite extra discount tiers.
- 10,000: grow proven sources while monitoring quality, engagement and actual product capacity. Do not hold a usable product back merely to hit the count.

A transparent share link can exist from launch without a reward engine. Later referral rewards should be limited, relevant and based on verified genuine users; never reward Product Hunt/other platform votes.

## Measurement and spend

Minimum weekly view: unique relevant landing visitors; raw and verified signups; qualification completion/known-fit share; beta interest; attributed qualified signups per source; unsubscribes/bounces; repeat replies/clicks; eventual invitation -> meaningful review -> repeated use -> paid conversion. Email opens alone are not evidence of purchase intent.

Denominators must be explicit. Use visitor-to-verified conversion, verified-to-known-qualified share, and cost per qualified subscriber. Qualification non-response is missing data. Keep acquisition reporting separate from actual product-outcome measurement in `docs/BETA_TELEMETRY_SPEC.md`.

Start organic-first. No paid-media budget, listing fee, tool subscription or binding promotion is authorised by this document. Paid experiments require an approved capped budget, a proven destination and a contribution-margin model incorporating the proposed early discount. Do not invent an acceptable acquisition cost from a hypothetical list price.

## Launch gate

Public announcement without a product link can happen before product beta. Deliberate linked traffic waits for the public funnel to be safe and truthful. Minimum evidence includes functioning signup and email lifecycle; mobile readability; source attribution; privacy and offer wording; no live-data demo leakage; no unsupported capability claim; and closure or formally approved containment of the recorded historic credential issue.

The candidate palette does not need to be deployed across the whole app. A clean, isolated page can use the approved current identity, or a separately approved small visual treatment. Removing an unsafe public demo is acceptable; pretending it is fixed is not.

The current `PUBLIC_TRAFFIC_GATE.md` is still OPEN. Some items in that historic gate may be stale; reconcile them with actual evidence instead of blindly trusting either its checked or unchecked boxes. No gate is marked passed by this strategy.

If using a separate waitlist-only deployment, verify isolation, keys, redirects, links and data flow, then explicitly approve the bounded public surface. Moving the signup path is not automatically proof that an old exposed credential is contained.
